The **Netherlands regional market index** lists the 14 Dutch entries in the [[atlas-top-400-europe|Top 400 Regional Markets — Europe]] set — the smallest of the seven country breakout pages by entry count, built alongside France, Italy, and Poland as new pages rather than a repurposed stub. Each entry is a named suburb or satellite municipality between 15 and 80 kilometres of a major Dutch (or, in one case, cross-border Belgian) metropolitan reference point that contains at least one retail co-location cluster, qualified under the [[about-regional-markets-system|co-location regional market framework]]. This page is a filtered, Netherlands-specific view of that continental table — not a separate dataset — so every entry and tier count below matches the continental table exactly and updates automatically if that table is corrected. No rank or score is published for any market.

## Metropolitan groupings

The Netherlands' 14 markets cluster most heavily around Rotterdam (4 markets) and Nijmegen (3), with Eindhoven and The Hague each contributing 2. The list also includes one cross-border entry — Maastricht, whose nearest major metropolitan reference point is Liège, just across the Belgian border. Groningen and Amsterdam each anchor a single entry.

## Tier composition

's-Gravenhage (The Hague's formal name, suburb of Rotterdam) is the Netherlands' only entry combining a Tier 1 and a Tier 2 cluster. [[nl-breda|Breda]] is the only entry combining a Tier 1 and a Tier 3 cluster. Zwolle and Arnhem are the two entries combining a Tier 2 and a Tier 3 cluster, with no Tier 1 cluster. The remaining ten markets — including Maastricht and Roermond — qualify on a single Tier 1 cluster.
