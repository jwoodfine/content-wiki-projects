The **Germany regional market index** ranks German cities and regional centres outside the
seven primary commercial markets (Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Cologne, Frankfurt, Stuttgart,
Düsseldorf). Each meets the population, income, and retail infrastructure thresholds established
by the [[about-regional-markets-system|co-location regional market framework]]. Germany's federal
structure and historically polycentric urban system produce an unusually large number of
qualifying regional markets. Mid-sized cities with independent economic bases, high household
incomes, and established retail investment are distributed across all sixteen Bundesländer.

## National context

Germany's commercial property market outside the Big Seven is characterised by:

**High income floor.** German regional markets generally exhibit household disposable income
above the OECD median for their size cohort, reflecting the country's high minimum wage,
strong trade union wage agreements, and deep manufacturing employment base in mid-sized
cities.

**Fachmarktzentrum retail format.** The German equivalent of the North American [[power-centres|power centre]]
— a retail park combining DIY (Baumarkt), electronics, food, and home furnishings anchors —
is a consistent feature of qualifying German regional markets. The presence of OBI, Bauhaus,
or Hornbach alongside a Kaufland or Real hypermarket confirms the income and demand conditions
required for professional centre [[co-location-strategy|co-location]].

**Correction (2026-08-02):** the "Real" hypermarket chain was sold off by Metro AG
starting 2020, had its stores rebranded to Kaufland/Globus/Edeka/Rewe/V-Markt by
mid-2022, and the residual "real,-"-branded rump went bankrupt and was liquidated in
2024 — as of this page's own `last_edited` date, "Real hypermarket" is not a live,
current retail signal. **Flagged, not resolved.**

**Planning environment.** German retail planning is more restrictive than North American
equivalents: large-format retail requires specific designation in development plans. This
restricts Fachmarktzentrum formation to markets with sufficient demand history, making
existing retail park investment a stronger confirmation signal than in less-regulated markets.

## Coverage by Bundesland

**Bavaria** — markets in the Munich metropolitan region beyond the urban core (Ingolstadt,
[[de-by-augsburg|Augsburg]], Regensburg, Landshut), the Franconia region (Nuremberg excluded as a major-seven
adjacent market; Erlangen, Bamberg, Bayreuth as primary targets), and Eastern Bavaria.

**Baden-Württemberg** — markets in the Rhine-Neckar region outside Mannheim and Heidelberg
primaries, the Lake Constance region (Ravensburg-Weingarten, Friedrichshafen), and the
mid-size cities of the Swabian Alb.

**North Rhine-Westphalia** — markets outside the Cologne-Düsseldorf-Ruhr primary zone,
concentrated in Eastern Westphalia ([[de-nw-bielefeld|Bielefeld]], Paderborn, Gütersloh), Münsterland, and the
Siegerland.

**Lower Saxony and Bremen surrounds** — Braunschweig, Göttingen, Hildesheim, Osnabrück.

**Eastern Germany** — Dresden (Big Seven adjacent; secondary markets only), [[de-th-erfurt|Erfurt]], Magdeburg,
Rostock, and the Saxony-Anhalt regional centres.

**Correction (2026-07-16):** of the five previously-listed sample markets, only
[[de-rp-kaiserslautern|Kaiserslautern]] (rank 4, suburb of Mannheim) actually appears among

of Augsburg"), never as standalone entries themselves. This doesn't mean these cities aren't
real German markets — it means they are not currently ranked as TOP400 entries in their own
right; the Bundesland-coverage prose above is left as qualitative regional description
rather than rewritten, but should not be read as implying these named cities are themselves
TOP400 entries.
