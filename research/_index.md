This category will carry five flagship long-form research papers once the platform's research-render section is in place. Each paper is a complete argument, argued end to end, and is added deliberately rather than accumulated incrementally — this is a small, curated set, not an open-ended corpus. Every paper is a pre-peer-review working draft and, once published here, will carry the mandatory preprint notice that governs how working-draft research is presented on this wiki.

None of the five papers below are live yet. They are listed here in prose, without links, so a reader knows what is coming before the render pipeline that will host them is ready.

## Papers forthcoming for this wiki

- **Open-Source Building-Systems Data Layers for Urban-Scale Site Analysis.** A coverage assessment of eight AEC-relevant geospatial data-layer categories — building code climate zones, flood hazard designations, seismic design categories, solar irradiance, and landscape eco-regions among them — across sixteen countries in North America and Europe, evaluating each source by regulatory prescriptiveness, licence, and practical integration method.

- **The Commuter Archetype.** An analysis of car-rental branch clustering as an open-data proxy for identifying transit-adjacent commercial co-location at regional rail stations and airports, applied across eighteen countries and characterising the rail-to-airport distribution of the pattern.

- **Industrial Co-location in the Metropolitan Ring.** A definition and continental-scale characterisation of the Urban Fringe archetype — trades supply, hardware retail, equipment rental, and building materials clustering in the metropolitan ring without a grocery hypermarket anchor — implemented and validated across eighteen countries.

- **Top 600 Regional Markets.** A country-normalized scoring system for ranking suburban-regional retail co-location markets across North America and Europe from open-data spatial sources, supporting multi-country development-site candidate identification.

- **Coordination at Scale in Multi-Archive AI-Assisted Development Environments.** An observational case study of coordination mechanisms — decentralized publication gates, per-archive branch isolation, and automated inter-archive message relay — in a production multi-archive development environment.
