In management's view, Canadian shopping centres are increasingly designed to strengthen their connection to how customers live, work, and play — a trend that keeps customers on site longer. Woodfine identifies a convergence between this design trend, the underutilized [[power-centre-land-availability|parking land]] available at some Power Centres, and the shortage of leasable space suited to professional and industrial tenants. Woodfine's development is built to capture all three at once.

## The live/work/play trend

A significant part of the live/work/play shift is the availability of everyday services on site, not only goods for sale. A [[power-centre-co-location-thesis|Power Centre]] built purely for retail transactions gives customers fewer reasons to stay once a purchase is made. A Power Centre that also offers the professional and civic services customers need day to day gives them a reason to return, and to stay longer once they arrive.

## Extended-hours occupancy

National Retailers draw their peak traffic outside standard business hours — evenings and weekends, when shoppers are free to visit. Woodfine's developments are intended to supply 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. occupancy on the same site, introducing a professional workforce whose hours complement rather than compete with retail's own peak periods.

This pattern gives the Power Centre two occupancy profiles instead of one. A professional workforce present on weekday mornings and afternoons generates its own foot traffic during hours the retail anchors are comparatively quiet, and it integrates with, rather than displaces, the retail operations already on site.

## Why the three trends converge on one site

Each trend reinforces the others at the same physical location. The live/work/play design shift creates demand for on-site services; underutilized parking land supplies the site to build them on; and the shortage of institutional-grade professional space in [[regional-market-capital-gap|Regional Markets]] means that demand has been going unmet. Woodfine's [[power-centre-co-location-thesis|co-located]] professional buildings are designed to resolve all three conditions from a single Development Site rather than treating them as separate problems.
