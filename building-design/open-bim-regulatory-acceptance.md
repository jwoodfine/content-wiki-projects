Building Information Modelling is no longer elective in public procurement for most G7 economies. The United States federal government, the European Union, the United Kingdom, and a growing list of other jurisdictions have each mandated open, vendor-neutral BIM data formats — principally IFC (ISO 16739-1:2024) — rather than any single vendor's proprietary format. Building against open standards from the outset keeps a development eligible for public-sector and institutional work that requires it, without a later retrofit.

## Why open standards, not proprietary formats

Open BIM means model data is exchanged in vendor-neutral formats that any conformant tool can read, rather than a format tied to one software vendor's continued existence. Government procurement rules increasingly require this distinction explicitly: a development specified against IFC and related open standards satisfies public-sector and institutional requirements as a matter of course, rather than requiring conversion or rework when a project later needs to meet them.

## What this means for a developer

For an institutional or public-sector-adjacent development, working against open standards from the start avoids two real costs: the compliance risk of discovering late that a mandate applies, and the conversion cost of retrofitting a proprietary-format project to an open one. Woodfine's buildings are designed and specified against open standards throughout, so this compliance question does not arise as a late-stage risk.
