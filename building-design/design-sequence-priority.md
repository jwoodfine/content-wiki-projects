Woodfine sequences its design collaborators in a fixed order — Leasing Plan first, then Building Services, then Structural — so that each discipline designs against a completed prior layer rather than negotiating in parallel with the others. The order is not a scheduling convenience; it is the mechanism Woodfine relies on to keep any one collaborator from redesigning the building around their own discipline's convenience once construction is already committed.

## The three-step order

[[key-plans-and-tiles|Key Plans and Tiles]] come first because they are the Leasing Plan — the available leaseholds a Woodfine building is built to sell. Once the Key Plans and Tiles are fixed, Building Services is overlaid against them, sizing distribution and service zones to leaseholds that are already known rather than to a floor plate that might still change. Structural comes last: only once Building Services has been overlaid on the Key Plans and Tiles can a Structural Collaborator identify which structural system — concrete, steel, timber, or hybrid — actually fits the resulting Tenant Mix and leasehold sizing.

## What it replaces

The order replaces an earlier practice of engaging Structural or Building Services before the Leasing Plan was settled. That practice deferred to whichever discipline happened to be under contract first, or to the "convenience of design" a linear structural grid offers a Structural Collaborator regardless of what the leaseholds actually needed. The failure mode is direct, and common across commercial development. A tenant's designers first engage with the Key Plans only after the building is already built. By then the Key Plans can shape the fit-out of one leasehold and nothing more; what they could have meant for the design of the building itself is already fixed. Correcting that at this stage means rebuilding rather than redrawing. The sequence is fixed in advance for exactly that reason, rather than negotiated project by project.

## Why leasing sets the terms, not the reverse

A [[fixed-floor-plates|Fixed Floor Plate]] whose demising walls fall at [[key-plans-and-tiles|Tile]] boundaries is only column-free at the leasehold level if the structural grid was placed around the Tiles — not the other way around. Sequencing Leasing Plan first is what makes that possible: a Structural Collaborator working from a completed Key Plans and Tiles layer places columns in line with leasehold boundaries, rather than fitting leaseholds around a grid chosen for beam economy. The same logic governs [[tile-level-climate-zoning|Tile-level climate zoning]], where Building Services can size a thermostat zone to a Tile only once the Tile itself is fixed. It also governs [[structural-bay-depth-discipline|structural bay depth]], which depends on knowing what a leasehold needs before a beam span is chosen to serve it.

## Consequence for construction discipline

The sequence is also what lets Woodfine avoid depending on any single collaborator to hold the whole design together. Because each layer is complete before the next collaborator begins, no one discipline can force a redesign of an earlier layer without an explicit decision to reopen it. That discipline is what allows a [[fixed-floor-plates|Fixed Floor Plate]] design, developed once per prototype class, to be reproduced across multiple Development Sites without re-negotiating the same sequencing questions on every deployment.
