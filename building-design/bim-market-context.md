Government Building Information Modelling (BIM) mandates now govern public construction across the UK, the EU, and a growing list of other jurisdictions — requiring open, vendor-neutral data formats rather than any single software vendor's proprietary file. Woodfine's buildings are designed and specified against these open standards from the outset, so a building's design, construction, and operational records remain usable and transferable long after any particular software tool is retired.

## Open standards, not vendor lock-in

A building record locked to one vendor's proprietary format loses value the moment that vendor's software changes course, is discontinued, or raises its price. Woodfine's approach builds on the Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) standard — published as ISO 16739-1:2024 and already required by government BIM mandates in the UK, most of the EU, and a growing list of other markets. A building's records are designed to stay portable and independently readable across every software tool that touches them, regardless of which vendors come and go over a building's decades-long life.

## The Golden Thread example

The UK's Building Safety Act 2022 illustrates the stakes directly. It requires a continuously maintained "Golden Thread" of safety-critical information for higher-risk buildings — from first design through occupation — that stays with the building through every change of ownership. Woodfine's building records are designed to satisfy this kind of continuous, transferable record-keeping requirement natively, not as a retrofit.

## Where the specification lives

The BIM Object Library, Key Plan methodology, and technical implementation detail behind this approach are published and maintained directly at [bim.woodfinegroup.com](https://bim.woodfinegroup.com) — the object catalog, standards mapping, and specification detail belong there, built up over time, rather than duplicated here.
